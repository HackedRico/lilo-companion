# Lilo demo script

For the LILO Summer Academy Hackathon submission. Track 01, LILO Behind the
Scenes: DSA practice, résumé screening, interviewing. Demo video is three
minutes maximum, and the brief is to show what you built and how you used the
track. This is about 350 words spoken, which lands under the cutoff with room
for the on-screen waits.

## Cold open

**Screen:** just the orb in the corner, panel shut.

> Every CS student has said it. "I'm never going to use this." Lilo is a
> desktop companion that proves them wrong, and then sits beside them while
> they do the work anyway.

## Name the track

**Do:** open the panel.

> We built Track One: DSA practice and interviewing. But both of those start
> with a question a student asks before either of them, so that is where I will
> start too.

## Closing the academia gap

**Do:** upload a lecture.

> Here is the gap itself. I hand it a lecture.
>
> It names the concept, tells me what industry calls the same thing, and quotes
> a real job posting to prove it. Samsara. Security engineer. That's a sentence
> from a live posting with a URL behind it, not a model's opinion.

**Do:** upload something it can't map, like a binary trees lecture.

> And when a concept genuinely isn't advertised for, it says so, instead of
> inventing a link.

## DSA practice

> That's the gap. Now the two things Track One names, starting with the grind.

**Screen:** Chrome, LeetCode problem open, panel already showing the code.

> It's reading my editor through a Chrome extension. No copy-paste, no
> uploading my code anywhere.

**Do:** press **Give me a hint**.

> I'm stuck, so I ask. Watch what it doesn't do.

**Do:** let the hint land.

> It asks me a question. It won't hand me the answer, because I set the ceiling
> to Coach. And that ceiling is enforced in code, not politely requested of the
> model. Every hint carries a rung, and one that reaches higher than my setting
> gets refused before I ever see it.

**Do:** break the code, hit Run, get the wrong answer.

> It read the verdict straight off the page.

**Do:** type "why is it returning that?"

> It named the actual bug. I still type the fix myself.

## Interviewing

**Do:** type "what is the interview at Google like?"

> Then the interview itself. This isn't guessing. It's reading write-ups people
> actually posted, and every claim is a citation I can click through to the
> original. It even tells me how old those accounts are, because a write-up
> from last year is worth reading differently.
>
> That's the difference between advice and evidence.

## Close

**Screen:** back to the orb.

> Everything it claims about industry traces to a real posting or a real
> write-up. The model translates. The postings prove.
>
> What your classes are actually worth. What the interview really looks like.
> And someone beside you on the grind who never takes the rep.
>
> That's Lilo.

## Before recording

- Drag the panel bigger by its corner. Judges need to read the quoted posting.
- Have the LeetCode tab open, and give the extension twenty seconds if the app
  was just restarted.
- Turn the voice toggle off, or the header reads VOICE ON for three minutes
  advertising a microphone that has no transcriber configured.
- Ask one throwaway question first. The first model call after launch is the
  slow one; every call after it answers in two to five seconds.

## If it runs long

Cut the binary trees beat. It is the most interesting thing in the demo and the
only one a judge will not miss.

## On résumé screening

Track 01 names three student challenges and this covers two of them. The gap
recap is the nearest thing to the third, and it is not a résumé screener. Two
done properly reads better than three claimed loosely.
